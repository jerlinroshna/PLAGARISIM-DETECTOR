1. What is Academic Integrity?

Academic integrity is the commitment to fundamental values of honesty, trust, fairness, respect, and responsibility in scholarly work. In computer science, this requires that all code submitted represents your individual understanding, design, and implementation.

2. What is Code Plagiarism?

Code plagiarism involves presenting someone else’s programming logic, syntax, or algorithm as your own without explicit authorization and citation. This includes copying solutions from peers, internet repositories, or past academic years.

3. How Does Automated Similarity Screening Work?

When you submit code, it is processed statically through two complementary analyzers: (1) Token-level winnowing to check sequence overlap, and (2) Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) structural parsing to compare grammar and control-flow patterns against historical benchmark repositories.

4. What is Token Similarity? (40% Weight)

Token similarity extracts lexed code tokens, computes n-gram hashes, and applies a robust mathematical winnowing algorithm to detect shared sub-sequences of logic regardless of formatting.

5. What is AST Structural Similarity? (60% Weight)

The Abstract Syntax Tree captures the grammatical hierarchy of your program. It analyzes statement blocks, loop constructs, conditional branches, and arithmetic expressions independently of surface text.

6. Why Variable Renaming Does Not Bypass Structural Detection

During AST normalization, variable names, function names, and comments are mapped into standardized canonical identifiers (e.g. var_1 = var_2 + var_3). Renaming variables, modifying whitespace, or reordering commutative operations yields the exact same syntax tree node representation.

7. What Does a Similarity Score Mean?

A similarity score is an objective statistical measure of structural overlap. Scores are partitioned into Low (< 30%), Medium (30%–69%), and High (≥ 70%) bands. Standard algorithms often produce modest structural overlap naturally.

8. Why Faculty Review is Mandatory

The system never issues automated misconduct penalties. Automated metrics only serve as investigative screening alerts for course instructors, who evaluate contextual nuances and standard lab idioms.
